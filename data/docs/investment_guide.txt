INVESTMENT GUIDE – INVESTMENT EDUCATION KNOWLEDGE BASE

1. INTRODUCTION TO INVESTING

Investing involves allocating money to financial assets with the expectation of generating returns over time.

Common investment objectives include:
- Wealth creation
- Retirement planning
- Education funding
- Long-term financial security
- Protection against inflation

Every investment involves some degree of risk. Returns are not guaranteed.

2. TYPES OF INVESTMENTS

Stocks:
Stocks represent ownership shares in a company. Investors may earn returns through price appreciation and dividends. Stock prices can fluctuate significantly.

Bonds:
Bonds are debt instruments issued by governments or corporations. Investors generally receive interest payments and repayment according to the bond's terms, subject to issuer credit risk.

Mutual Funds:
Mutual funds pool money from multiple investors and invest in a portfolio of assets managed according to the fund's objectives.

Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs):
ETFs are investment funds traded on stock exchanges. They can provide diversified exposure to a basket of securities.

Fixed Deposits:
Fixed deposits allow individuals to deposit money with a bank for a specified period at an agreed interest rate, subject to the product's terms and applicable regulations.

Gold:
Gold is a commodity that investors may use as part of a diversified portfolio. Its market price can fluctuate.

Real Estate:
Real estate investments involve purchasing property to generate rental income or potential capital appreciation.

3. RISK AND RETURN

Risk refers to the possibility that an investment's actual return may differ from the expected return.

Return refers to the gain or loss generated by an investment over a specific period.

Generally, investments with higher expected returns may involve higher risks, but higher risk does not guarantee higher returns.

Examples:

- Savings accounts generally have lower volatility.
- Fixed deposits provide defined interest terms but involve inflation and institutional considerations.
- Equity investments can experience substantial price fluctuations.
- Cryptocurrency investments can involve extremely high volatility and significant loss risk.

4. DIVERSIFICATION

Diversification is the strategy of spreading investments across different assets, industries, and markets.

Benefits:
- Reduces concentration risk.
- Helps manage portfolio volatility.
- Reduces dependence on a single investment.
- Provides exposure to different market opportunities.

Diversification does not eliminate all investment risks or guarantee profits.

5. ASSET ALLOCATION

Asset allocation determines how investments are distributed among different asset classes.

Common asset classes include:
- Equity
- Debt instruments
- Cash and cash equivalents
- Gold
- Real estate

Asset allocation depends on:
- Financial goals
- Investment time horizon
- Risk tolerance
- Income stability
- Liquidity requirements
- Existing financial obligations

6. INVESTMENT TIME HORIZON

Short-Term Goals:
Financial goals expected within a relatively short period. Capital preservation and liquidity may be important.

Medium-Term Goals:
Financial goals that may require a balanced approach to growth, risk, and liquidity.

Long-Term Goals:
Goals such as retirement planning that may allow investors to consider long-term growth opportunities while managing volatility.

7. SIP – SYSTEMATIC INVESTMENT PLAN

A Systematic Investment Plan allows investors to invest a fixed amount regularly into a mutual fund.

Benefits:
- Encourages disciplined investing.
- Supports regular contributions.
- Can average purchase costs over time.
- Helps develop long-term investment habits.

SIP investments are subject to market risks. Returns are not guaranteed.

8. COMPOUNDING IN INVESTMENTS

Compounding occurs when investment earnings generate additional earnings over time.

The final investment value depends on:
- Initial investment.
- Additional contributions.
- Rate of return.
- Investment duration.
- Compounding frequency.
- Fees and taxes.

Longer investment periods can increase the impact of compounding, but market returns are uncertain.

9. INVESTMENT RISK FACTORS

Market Risk:
The possibility of investment losses due to changes in market prices.

Credit Risk:
The possibility that a borrower or issuer may fail to meet financial obligations.

Inflation Risk:
The risk that rising prices reduce the purchasing power of investment returns.

Liquidity Risk:
The risk that an asset cannot be sold quickly without a significant price reduction.

Interest Rate Risk:
The possibility that changes in interest rates affect the value of investments.

Concentration Risk:
The risk of having too much money invested in one asset, company, sector, or market.

10. BASIC INVESTMENT PRINCIPLES

- Define financial goals before investing.
- Understand the risks associated with each asset.
- Diversify investments appropriately.
- Avoid investing money needed for immediate expenses.
- Review investment performance periodically.
- Consider transaction costs, taxes, and fees.
- Avoid making investment decisions based only on social media recommendations.
- Never assume past returns guarantee future performance.
